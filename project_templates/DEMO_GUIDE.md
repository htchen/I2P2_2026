# Project Demo Guide

The demo evaluates demonstrated ownership of the submitted software. AI use
during development is permitted; the individual demo is AI-free.

## Team presentation

- Reproduce the submitted revision from a clean build.
- Demonstrate required behavior and one boundary or failure case.
- Show the architecture/ownership map and verification evidence.
- Identify known limitations and one AI suggestion that was rejected or repaired.

## Individual check

Each student should be prepared to:

1. explain one assigned function or subsystem;
2. trace data, control flow, and ownership through it;
3. interpret one test failure, compiler diagnostic, or sanitizer report;
4. justify one design or container choice;
5. make, compile, and verify a small change without AI or external network use.

The modification should be bounded and diagnostic of understanding—for example,
adding a validation case, changing a constant through the proper interface,
extending a test, or repairing a small seeded defect. It should not require a
new subsystem during the demo.

## Staff consistency checklist

- Use modifications of comparable scope across students.
- Ask about code the student claims as their contribution, then sample one
  integration boundary shared by the team.
- Distinguish nervous syntax mistakes from inability to explain the design.
- Record the question, evidence observed, and any individual grade adjustment.
